Science · TfR1 CapX™

A capsid reprogrammed
to use the brain's own door.

TfR1 CapX is an AAV capsid engineered to bind human transferrin receptor 1. Given as an intravenous infusion, it crosses the blood-brain barrier by the transport route the body runs continuously, and delivers its cargo throughout the central nervous system.

Why TfR1

Four decades of receptor biology, now carrying a capsid

Cells take up iron through TfR1, also called CD71 and encoded by TFRC. In the brain, the receptor sits on the endothelial cells lining the vessels, where it carries iron-loaded transferrin across the barrier by receptor-mediated transcytosis and then recycles to the surface to do it again.

That makes TfR1 a transport system the body is always running. Antibody-based brain shuttles have already used it in people: two TfR1-targeted enzyme therapies are approved for Hunter syndrome, and a TfR1 brain-shuttle antibody is in Phase 3 for early Alzheimer's disease. Apertura is the first to target human TfR1 with an AAV capsid.

Design decisions

What makes TfR1 CapX different

HUMAN-FIRST

Engineered on the human receptor

Selection ran against human TfR1 from the outset, rather than discovering a capsid in non-human primates and back-mapping it to human biology. The binding site is the apical domain — the same region engaged by antibody brain shuttles already dosed in people.

ROUTE

Intravenous, not surgical

Intrathecal, intracisternal and intraparenchymal dosing need a procedure, and the vector then spreads by diffusion — heaviest at the injection site. An infusion uses a vasculature that already reaches every region, with active transport instead of diffusion.

GENERATION 2

Built on BI-hTFR1, then improved

BI-hTFR1 is the first-generation TfR1-targeted capsid from Ben Deverman's lab at the Broad Institute, published in Science in 2024. TfR1 CapX is a second-generation capsid derived from it and further engineered for brain delivery efficiency.

SAFETY MARGIN

Normal iron handling continues

The capsid binds away from the transferrin site and binds equally well when the receptor already carries iron-loaded transferrin. Transferrin circulates far above a gene therapy dose and binds far more tightly, so ordinary receptor function is expected to continue.

POPULATION

Little room for genetic variation

The apical domain carries no known variants above roughly one in a thousand, and published work predicts on that basis that common variation in TFRC would not affect binding.

LIFESPAN

Transport holds across ages

A 2026 study measured TfR1-mediated transport in mice from the neonatal period to 24 months: elevated in neonates, then stable across adulthood with no decline at any later age studied. Clinically, TfR1 shuttles are in use from infancy to age 90.

Vasculature

To reach the brain, go through the blood vessels

No cell in a mammalian brain sits more than about 20 µm from a vessel, and all mammalian brains are similarly vascularized. An intravenous route therefore gives broad distribution and scales between species, which local administration cannot do.

Readiness

Advancing to the clinic

TfR1 CapX is expected to enter the clinic in several programs over the next 6–12 months, which would make it the leading IV-administered, BBB-crossing AAV capsid. That readiness rests on preclinical development, regulatory engagement, and established CDMO manufacturing — including a strategic manufacturing collaboration with Viralgen.
