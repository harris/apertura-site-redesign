Process

How it works

  1. ApplySubmit the intake form. We log the submission and begin review.
  2. ReviewWe check eligibility and completeness. If something is missing we ask you to update it; if your lab is not eligible, we discuss alternative access routes.
  3. AgreementsWe send your Technology Transfer Office a packet with a cover letter, a CDA and the MTA. If your institution already has an MTA with us, your lab is added as an authorized group.
  4. Materials & supportAfter execution, you receive the TfR1 CapX documentation package and introductions to vendor contacts.

Eligibility

Who qualifies

Institution. An accredited academic research entity.

Use. Non-commercial research only. Not for clinical administration.

People. A named principal investigator and a valid Technology Transfer Office contact.

Compliance. Agreement to the standard MTA terms and applicable regulations.

Final eligibility is determined during internal review. If you have no academic affiliation — for-profit, not-for-profit or foundation — write to us and we will discuss other access pathways.

Terms, plainly

What the standard MTA allows

Allowed

Use of TfR1 CapX materials for academic, non-commercial research.

Internal evaluation in the PI's lab and approved contracted parties, including CROs and CDMOs.

Publishing data, subject to a window that opens 18 months after execution or on the Broad Institute's TfR1 CapX publication, whichever comes first. The restriction protects our scientific founder's first publication, and rarely binds in practice: study design, execution, analysis and writing usually run longer than 18 months.

Limited data sharing for academic purposes under confidentiality obligations.

Not allowed

Clinical use, diagnostic use in humans, or manufacturing for human administration.

Commercial use, sublicensing, or redistribution to third parties.

Reverse engineering or sequence disclosure beyond what the MTA permits.

Use outside the approved indications or the labs named in the agreement.

Use in regulatory submissions without notifying Apertura first.

Program FAQ

Before you apply

Who can apply?

Principal investigators and research teams at accredited academic institutions conducting non-commercial research.

Is there a cost?

No. The program provides no-cost access to TfR1 CapX for eligible academic researchers.

My institution already has an MTA with Apertura. What then?

After review, your lab is added as an exhibit under the existing agreement.

What if my application is incomplete or not eligible?

We will ask you to complete the missing items, or, if the lab is not eligible, discuss alternative access options.

Can I include multiple indications in one application?

Yes — up to five indications or programs per submission, each with a brief development summary. Additional indications can be added at any time after the MTA is completed, by you or by other labs at your institution.

When will we hear back?

Timing depends mostly on your institution's review process. We move as quickly as we can on our side.

Can we publish results?

Generally yes, subject to the MTA's publication terms described above.

Is there a path to the clinic for a therapeutic using TfR1 CapX?

Yes. If you have promising results that you believe should advance, contact us to discuss a commercial license.
