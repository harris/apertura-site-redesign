Receptor biology

Transferrin receptor

What is the transferrin receptor (TfR1), and what does it do?

Cells use the transferrin receptor, TfR1, to take up iron. Iron moves through the blood bound to a carrier protein, transferrin; transferrin binds TfR1, which brings it into the cell, releases the iron, and cycles quickly back to the surface to repeat the process. Every cell needs iron, so the receptor is everywhere and the process runs continuously.

Cells in the brain need iron too, but transferrin is too large to cross the blood-brain barrier on its own. There, TfR1 ferries transferrin and iron across the barrier by receptor-mediated transcytosis and releases them on the brain side. The protein appears in the literature as TfR1, CD71, and the gene name TFRC.

ReferencesHentze MW et al. Two to tango: regulation of mammalian iron metabolism. Cell 2010;142:24–38. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20603012/Lawrence CM et al. Crystal structure of the ectodomain of human transferrin receptor. Science 1999;286:779–82. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10531064/Khan AI, Liu J, Dutta P. Iron transport kinetics through blood-brain barrier endothelial cells. BBA Gen Subj 2018;1862(5):1168–79.
What makes TfR1 a good target for getting therapeutics across the blood-brain barrier?

The barrier exists to keep large molecules, bacteria, fungi and viruses out, which is also why biologics struggle to reach the brain. Anything in the bloodstream that is too large to cross on its own has to use a receptor that is already part of the barrier.

TfR1 is found on the endothelial cells lining brain vessels, where it carries its cargo across and resets. A therapeutic that leverages TfR1 is using transport the body performs anyway. This is not a new idea: TfR1 has been studied for nearly 50 years, and medicines that use it to shuttle biologics into the brain are already approved, with others in late-stage trials.

ReferencesJefferies WA et al. Transferrin receptor on endothelium of brain capillaries. Nature 1984;312:162–3. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6095085/Pardridge WM. Receptor-mediated drug delivery of bispecific therapeutic antibodies through the blood-brain barrier. Front Drug Deliv 2023;3:1227816.
How does a therapeutic actually get across?

By receptor-mediated transcytosis — the same machinery that moves iron. A molecule binds TfR1 on the blood-facing surface of an endothelial cell; the cell draws receptor and cargo into an endosome; the endosome crosses the cell and releases the cargo into the brain; the receptor returns to the surface.

Therapeutics in development bind at the apical domain of the receptor, a different location from the transferrin site, and have been shown not to interfere with transferrin binding.

ReferencesCheng Y et al. Structure of the human transferrin receptor-transferrin complex. Cell 2004;116:565–76. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14980223/
Does TfR1 expression change with age?

TfR1 is present and functional at the barrier throughout life, and TfR1-mediated delivery has now been demonstrated clinically from infancy through older adulthood.

The most complete preclinical dataset is a 2026 study measuring TfR1-mediated transport in mice from the neonatal period to 24 months. Vascular TfR1 protein and brain uptake of a TfR1-targeted molecule were elevated in neonates, then stable across adulthood from 3 to 24 months, with no decline at any later age studied. Clinically, tividenofusp alfa (AVLAYAH) and pabinafusp alfa (IZCARGO) treat pediatric Hunter syndrome patients, while trontinemab is in Phase 3 in adults aged 50 to 90.

ReferencesTorres VO et al. J Cereb Blood Flow Metab 2026;46(1):20–35. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40817783/Muenzer J et al. An intravenous brain-penetrant enzyme therapy for mucopolysaccharidosis II. N Engl J Med 2026;394(1):39–50.
Have drugs that target TfR1 been given to people?

Yes, in both pediatric and geriatric populations. Two TfR1-targeted medicines are approved for Hunter syndrome: tividenofusp alfa received FDA accelerated approval in March 2026, and pabinafusp alfa has been approved in Japan since 2021. Trontinemab, an antibody engineered to bind TfR1, is in Phase 3 for Alzheimer's disease, with regular MRI monitoring and favorable safety and efficacy signals reported so far.

Those are enzyme-replacement therapies and antibodies given repeatedly, requiring sustained receptor interaction over months and years. An AAV gene therapy is intended as a single administration.

ReferencesFDA. FDA approves drug to treat neurologic manifestations of Hunter syndrome, 2026.Hoffmann-La Roche. Phase III trontinemab study, NCT07169578. https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07169578
Could genetic variation change how much TfR1 a person has?

Complete loss of TfR1 is not compatible with life, so the TFRC variants seen in people reduce or alter function rather than eliminate it. One rare inherited condition, caused by a specific change in both copies, produces an immune disorder; given the receptor's role in red cell development, those patients have mild anemia. Population databases show TFRC is only moderately constrained, below the conventional threshold at which losing one copy is expected to cause disease.

ReferencesLevy JE et al. Nat Genet 1999;21:396–9. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10192390/Jabara HH et al. Nat Genet 2016;48:74–8. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26642240/

The capsid

TfR1 CapX

What is TfR1 CapX and how does it work?

An AAV capsid engineered to bind human transferrin receptor 1, so it can be given as an intravenous infusion and carried across the blood-brain barrier by receptor-mediated transcytosis. It binds the apical domain of the receptor — the same region engaged by antibody-based brain shuttles already dosed in people, and away from the transferrin binding site, so normal TfR1 function is not expected to be impaired.

ReferencesHuang Q et al. Science 2024;384:1220–7. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38753766/
Why intravenous delivery rather than direct CNS administration of AAV9?

Direct CNS administration — intrathecal, intracisternal or intraparenchymal — requires a surgical procedure, and the vector then spreads largely by diffusion from wherever it was placed, so distribution is uneven and strongest at the injection site.

An intravenous infusion requires no surgery, uses a vasculature that already reaches every part of the brain, and crosses the barrier by active transport rather than diffusion from a single point.

Are TfR1 CapX and BI-hTFR1 the same thing?

Related, but not the same. BI-hTFR1 is the first-generation TfR1-targeted capsid described in the 2024 Science paper from Ben Deverman's laboratory at the Broad Institute. TfR1 CapX is a second-generation capsid based on it, engineered further to increase brain delivery efficiency.

ReferencesChan KY et al. BI-TfR1 CapX rapidly transits the blood-brain barrier for efficient, low-dose gene delivery throughout the CNS. bioRxiv, posted 29 September 2026.
Does TfR1 CapX interfere with normal TfR1 function?

It binds a region away from the transferrin site, and binds equally well when the receptor is already carrying iron-loaded transferrin. Iron uptake is not blocked. Transferrin is present in blood at levels far above the dose level of a gene therapy and binds the receptor far more tightly than TfR1-binding AAVs, so ordinary receptor function is expected to continue regardless.

ReferencesCheng Y et al. Cell 2004;116:565–76. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14980223/
Has anyone been treated with a TfR1-targeted AAV gene therapy? When will TfR1 CapX be in the clinic?

Not yet — no AAV gene therapy targeting the transferrin receptor has been given to a person. TfR1 CapX is expected to enter the clinic in several programs over the next 6–12 months.

Would genetic variation in TFRC affect how well TfR1 CapX works?

The apical domain, which the capsid binds, carries no known variants above a population frequency of about one in a thousand. Published work predicts on that basis that common genetic variation would not affect binding.

ReferencesHuang Q et al. Science 2024;384:1220–7. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38753766/
